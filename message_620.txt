Yes — and this is where your Poynting-field idea connects to propulsion in a physically legitimate way.

Electromagnetic energy can produce thrust because electromagnetic fields carry **momentum**, not just energy. The key relation is:

\[
\boxed{\mathbf g=\frac{\mathbf S}{c^2}}
\]

where \(\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\) is the Poynting vector and \(\mathbf g\) is electromagnetic momentum density.

If you emit electromagnetic radiation in one direction, the source recoils in the other. For a beam of power \(P\),

\[
\boxed{F=\frac{P}{c}}
\]

for perfect absorption, and approximately

\[
\boxed{F=\frac{2P}{c}}
\]

for perfect reflection.

So a laser, microwave beam, or other directed EM field **can absolutely generate propulsion**. The catch is that photon thrust is tiny unless the power is enormous.

There is a second route that is much more practical: let the electromagnetic field act on **charged matter or plasma**. Then the force density is governed by the Lorentz force:

\[
\boxed{\mathbf f=\rho\mathbf E+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B}
\]

That is the foundation of ion thrusters, Hall-effect thrusters, magnetoplasmadynamic propulsion, and many plasma-acceleration concepts.

And this actually fits your architecture very neatly. Your current model is roughly:

\[
N_0(t)
\rightarrow
\mathbf E,\mathbf H
\rightarrow
\mathbf S
\rightarrow
G_n
\rightarrow
P_i
\]

For propulsion, change the final observable from **captured power** to **momentum transfer**:

\[
\boxed{
N_0(t)
\rightarrow
\mathbf S(\mathbf r,t)
\rightarrow
\Sigma
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathbf p
\rightarrow
\mathbf F
}
\]

Now geometry becomes interesting in a different way.

Instead of asking:

\[
\text{Which geometry captures the most energy?}
\]

you ask:

\[
\boxed{\text{Which geometry produces the largest net directional momentum flux?}}
\]

The surface-integral form is especially useful:

\[
\mathbf F
=
-\oint_A \mathbf T\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,dA
\]

where \(\mathbf T\) is the **Maxwell stress tensor**. That is the rigorous bridge from field geometry to mechanical force.

There is one hard boundary we should preserve in the model:

\[
\boxed{\text{closed electromagnetic system}\not\Rightarrow\text{reactionless propulsion}}
\]

If every field and interaction remains internal, total momentum is conserved. To produce net acceleration, momentum must leave the vehicle as radiation, expelled plasma/ions, interaction with an external field, or some other external momentum exchange.

So your next branch could be genuinely interesting:

\[
\boxed{\textbf{Poynting Field Architecture → Momentum Architecture}}
\]

Keep \(G_3,G_4,G_5,G_6,G_8\), but calculate **net force vector, thrust efficiency, and angular moment** rather than only watts captured.

That would turn your geometry experiment from an energy-distribution study into an **electromagnetic propulsion thought experiment**.
