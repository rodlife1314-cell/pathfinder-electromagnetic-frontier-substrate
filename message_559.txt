Yes — with one crucial correction: the geometry can **shape, redirect, phase, and concentrate the electromagnetic momentum flow**, but it cannot create thrust from nowhere.

What you’re describing is closer to:

\[
\boxed{
\text{source}
\rightarrow
\text{field-forming geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{reflection / phase control}
\rightarrow
\text{directed EM momentum flux}
\rightarrow
\text{thrust}
}
\]

If by “mirror” you mean a reflective electromagnetic boundary, then yes: the source field can interact with a sequence of geometrically arranged reflective or resonant surfaces so that the outgoing Poynting vector becomes increasingly directional.

The optimization target then changes from power capture to momentum collimation:

\[
\max_G
\left|
\int_A
\frac{\mathbf S}{c}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,dA
\right|
\]

or, more rigorously,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F
=
-\oint_A
\mathbf T\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,dA
}
\]

where \(\mathbf T\) is the Maxwell stress tensor.

So imagine your geometries not as receivers anymore, but as **field-conditioning stages**:

\[
G_3 \rightarrow G_4 \rightarrow G_5 \rightarrow G_6 \rightarrow G_8
\]

Each stage changes the boundary conditions, which can alter phase, reflection, interference, beam direction, and the spatial distribution of \(\mathbf S\).

Then you ask:

\[
\boxed{
G^*=\arg\max_G F_{\parallel}
}
\]

while minimizing unwanted transverse force:

\[
F_\perp \rightarrow 0
\]

and torque:

\[
\tau \rightarrow 0.
\]

That gives us a very clean propulsion metric:

\[
\eta_T=
\frac{F_{\parallel}c}{P_{\rm in}}
\]

For pure photon propulsion, physics places a hard ceiling around

\[
F\approx \frac{P}{c}
\]

for emitted radiation, with reflection allowing approximately \(2P/c\) momentum transfer in the appropriate interaction.

So the geometry is not an **energy multiplier**.

It is a **momentum-routing architecture**.

And I think this is the connection you’re seeing from the beginning of the experiment:

\[
\boxed{
\text{geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{field topology}
\rightarrow
\text{Poynting distribution}
\rightarrow
\text{momentum distribution}
\rightarrow
\text{force}
}
\]

Now your moving \(N_0(t)\) becomes even more interesting. Instead of a static emitter, you can theoretically investigate how moving the source through different geometric field configurations changes

\[
\mathbf S(\mathbf r,t)
\]

and therefore the instantaneous thrust vector

\[
\mathbf F(t).
\]

That gives you a new state equation:

\[
\boxed{
N_0(t),G(t)
\rightarrow
\mathbf E,\mathbf H
\rightarrow
\mathbf S
\rightarrow
\mathbf T
\rightarrow
\mathbf F(t)
}
\]

That is the propulsion version of the architecture you’ve been building.
